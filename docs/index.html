On efficacy of Meta-Learning for Domain Generalization in Speech Emotion Recognition

Why emotion models struggle beyond their training domain

Raeshak King Gandhi · Vasileios Tsouvalas · Nirvana Meratnia

At a glance

An emotion model can fit one speech corpus and fail on another. DA-MLDG rehearses source-domain shifts during training and weights them with an energy-gap estimate. The study finds mixed generalization gains and exposes limits of the representations and benchmarks.

Abstract

Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) refers to the recognition of human emotions from natural speech, vital for building human-centered context-aware intelligent systems. Here, domain shift, where models’ trained on one domain exhibit performance degradation when exposed to an unseen domain with different statistics, is a major limiting factor in SER applicability, as models have a strong dependence on speakers and languages characteristics used during training. Meta-Learning for Domain Generalization (MLDG) has shown great success in improving models’ generalization capacity and alleviate the domain shift problem in the vision domain; yet, its’ efficacy on SER remains largely unexplored. In this work, we propose a “domain-shift aware” MLDG approach to learn generalizable models across multiple domains in SER. Based on our extensive evaluation, we identify a number of pitfalls that contribute to poor models’ DG ability, and demonstrate that log-mel spectrograms representations lack distinct features required for MLDG in SER. We further explore the use of appropriate features to achieve DG in SER as to provide insides to future research directions for DG in SER.

How it works

DA-MLDG

01

Simulate domain shifts within sources

Partition source domains into disjoint sets S′ and V at each iteration. A gradient step on S′ produces adapted parameters θ′.

θ′=θ−ηs∇θℒθ(S′)

§III, Eq. 2 and Algorithm 1. V is a source meta-test subset, not the final target test corpus.

02

Use energy as a shift proxy

Compute each subset’s average log-sum-exp of logits with θ′ and temperature 1. The absolute gap supplies γ, the adaptive weight.

γ=|E(S′,pθ′)−E(V,pθ′)|

Eq. 3, printed p. 423. The score is proposed as a proxy for domain shift, not a measured distance between speech distributions.

03

Optimize the weighted outer objective

Minimize source training loss plus γ times meta-test loss after adaptation. The outer optimization accounts for how θ′ depends on θ.

minθℒθ(S′)+γℒθ′(V)

Eq. 2. The formula presents the stated objective rather than Algorithm 1’s outer-update line, which omits an explicit gradient symbol.

04

Evaluate representation and transfer together

Compare ResNet-18 with log-mel inputs against a variant using TRILLsson embeddings. Seven source/target scenarios cover same-language and cross-language transfer.

Tables I, III and IV; ten trials per setting. The target corpora remain unseen during training.

Takeaways

  • Performance depends on the source–target split: DA-MLDG leads two of seven log-mel scenarios.
  • Pretrained features change the comparison: DA-MLDG beats supervised training in three of seven scenarios.
