On efficacy of Meta-Learning for Domain Generalization in Speech Emotion Recognition

Why emotion models struggle beyond their training domain

Raeshak King Gandhi · Vasileios Tsouvalas · Nirvana Meratnia

PaperCodePoster
OverviewMethodResultsCitation
The challenge

Speech emotion classifiers lose accuracy when the test corpus, speakers or language differ from the training domains.

The contribution

DA-MLDG weights the meta-test loss by an energy-based estimate of domain shift and studies why this remains insufficient for practical speech emotion generalization.

Method overview · Based on the paper

The idea

At a glance

Models that recognize emotion in one speech dataset often fail on another. This study evaluates domain-shift-aware meta-learning for speech emotion recognition, finds mixed gains with log-mel inputs, and tests whether stronger acoustic representations improve held-out-domain performance.

  • Success of meta-learning in image domain generalization does not automatically transfer to speech emotion recognition.
  • Changing the representation can help ordinary supervised training as well as meta-learning.
  • The paper’s main contribution includes identifying evaluation and representation limitations, not claiming that domain shift has been solved.

  • Meta-learning
  • Speech emotion recognition
Read the abstract

Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) refers to the recognition of human emotions from natural speech, vital for building human-centered context-aware intelligent systems. Here, domain shift, where models’ trained on one domain exhibit performance degradation when exposed to an unseen domain with different statistics, is a major limiting factor in SER applicability, as models have a strong dependence on speakers and languages characteristics used during training. Meta-Learning for Domain Generalization (MLDG) has shown great success in improving models’ generalization capacity and alleviate the domain shift problem in the vision domain; yet, its’ efficacy on SER remains largely unexplored. In this work, we propose a “domain-shift aware” MLDG approach to learn generalizable models across multiple domains in SER. Based on our extensive evaluation, we identify a number of pitfalls that contribute to poor models’ DG ability, and demonstrate that log-mel spectrograms representations lack distinct features required for MLDG in SER. We further explore the use of appropriate features to achieve DG in SER as to provide insides to future research directions for DG in SER.

The approach

How it works

  1. 01

    Simulate unseen domains

    Split source-domain batches into non-overlapping meta-train and meta-test sets. Take a training step on the meta-train set before evaluating transfer to the held-out source domains.

  2. 02

    Estimate the domain shift

    Compute the difference between average energy scores on the two sets after the meta-train step. This margin acts as an adaptive measure of the shift between domains.

  3. 03

    Adapt the meta-objective

    Weight the meta-test loss by the estimated shift and optimize it together with the meta-train loss. Evaluate transfer to target corpora that were unavailable during training.

Experiments & evidence

What the results show

Read each target scenario separately: improvements over ordinary supervised training are mixed, and the strongest method changes with the scenario.

Data
Ten SER corpora: IEMOCAP, CREMA-D, SAVEE, RAVDESS, TESS, AESDD, CaFE, EMOVO, ShEMO and EmoDB.
Models
ResNet-18 with VoxCeleb pretraining; log-mel inputs or TRILLsson embeddings. A separate PACS image-domain comparison uses ImageNet initialization.
Compared with
Deep-All supervised training, vanilla MLDG with fixed weight, and Representation Self-Challenging (RSC).
Protocol
Seven corpus-transfer scenarios; five selected emotions; ten trials. Target corpora are held out from training.

Scope & limitations

  • The authors conclude that the achieved target-domain performance is insufficient for practical use; improvements are not consistent across baselines or scenarios.
  • Limited and largely acted corpora restrict coverage of natural speech variation; emotion categories can overlap acoustically.
  • ShEMO has no disgust examples, creating an imbalanced evaluation in scenarios 6 and 7.
  • The main comparison tables omit trial dispersion, limiting conclusions about small differences; t-SNE illustrations do not establish a causal explanation for failures.
